Faithful Clone Framework

How the agent turns one proven competitor ad into the same ad for our brand: same layout, type, photography and rhythm; our product, logo, colours, words and a new person. Built from a forensic review of every clone the agent has made (21 for Vital Vogue, 196 for PushOwl), Farooq's template libraries (the 140, the 459 and the 142 reel templates), the Winning Statics pipeline and current research.

What faithful meansSame ad, different brand. Written as rules a computer can check.

Stays the same (measured)Changes (allow-list only)
  • Canvas, crop and every element's box (layout grid)
  • The inventory: how many products, people, text blocks, icons, badges, rules and panels
  • Reading order and stacking order
  • Typography: font class (serif, sans, script, condensed, slab), weight, italic, case, size ratios between lines, tracking, alignment, line breaks, colour role and treatment (strikethrough, outline, highlight, repeated wall, rotation)
  • Photography: camera angle, lens feel, light direction and softness, set and props, pose and framing
  • Colour relationships: which areas are light or dark, which element is the accent
  • Copy architecture: each slot keeps its job (a price anchor stays a price anchor, a part label stays a part label), its line count and roughly its length
  • The product: our real packshot or catalogue garment, never one the model invents
  • The logo and any brand wordmark, in the source's logo position (or none if the source had none)
  • The source's own brand colours, swapped to ours by role (see decision D1)
  • The words: new copy per slot, from our claims ledger only
  • The person: a new identity, same pose, build, styling and crop
  • Nothing else. Any other difference is a defect.

Why this is stricter: research shows single-image vision judges are barely better than chance at spotting edit defects (the best tested model matched people's choices 59.6% of the time; in another study, 32% of the images a GPT judge passed had visible artifacts), while side-by-side comparison and decomposed yes or no checks agree with people far more often. So the judge can no longer give a holistic score: every dimension above is measured, and the vision model only gets narrow questions.

Decisions for youRecommendations first. Nothing is built until you approve.

D1 Colour. Recommended: swap by role. Only colours that carry the source brand's identity (its brand colour, accent and buttons) become ours; neutrals, backgrounds and photographic colours stay as in the source, and light and dark relationships never change. Alternative: keep the source palette exactly, with only our logo added.
D2 People. Recommended: a casting library per brand. 6 to 10 approved synthetic models per brand (generated once, approved by you), cast to match the source's build and styling. Alternatives: the brand's own model photos, or skip person-led sources.
D3 Copy. Recommended: every slot keeps its job. A price anchor shows our real price anchor (MRP struck through, real price), a size range shows our real sizes. If we lack a true equivalent, the slot is flagged for you, never invented and never swapped for a different message such as an offer.
D4 Fonts. Recommended: open-licence fonts matched by class first (serif, sans, script, condensed, slab, handwritten), with a paid font-identification service only if A3 shows class matching isn't enough. A brand's own fonts override when supplied.
D5 Human gates. Recommended: two. You approve each source's Clone Spec once (it then becomes a reusable template), and you approve finished ads. The gate itself never needs a human to pass an ad, only to accept one.
D6 Scope. Recommended: statics only, and no lingerie worn on a body, since the image models refuse it. Video clones come later on the same spec idea.
D7 Calibration. Recommended: you label about 50 pairs (about 20 minutes) so the thresholds match your eye; the round 2 renders and PushOwl's history give the first examples of unfaithful work.
