Today's generator, and why it has to change

The agent's search generator (google.js) works like this: seed keywords by intent, then price them with keyword volume, cluster, mine competitor Google ads, write 5 RSAs per ad group, and enforce claims and competitor rules. That order is right, but it was built for Brevo, not PushOwl installs:

  • Its seeds are Klaviyo alternatives and pricing, WooCommerce, WordPress and deliverability. The audit shows competitor terms at $593 per install, and non-Shopify terms can't install a Shopify app at all.
  • It lands on brevo.com pages. Installs only come from the listing.
  • It writes about 50 search ads a week that nothing launches, measures or learns from.

The maths that sets the rules

Cost per install = cost per click ÷ click-to-install rate. Your own history in the target countries shows 6% to 12% click-to-install on the listing, so:

Click-to-install rateMax cost per click for $20For the $25 ceiling
6%$1.20$1.50
9%$1.80$2.25
12% (the App Store campaign)$2.40$3.00

$20 is below every past Google result in these countries (the best was $33). It's reachable only on cheaper, high-intent Shopify terms, in the cheaper countries, on desktop. The agent prices every keyword against this cap before it's used, and says honestly when a theme can't hit $20.

Keyword research, the agent's way

Seed from winners and themes→Expand with Google's own keyword ideas→Price: volume, top-of-page bid range→Estimated CPI = bid ÷ theme's install rate→Keep under the $20 cap→Negatives from history→Ad groups
  • Seeds: your historical winners ("shopify email marketing", "abandoned checkout email", "sms shopify", "shopify best email marketing app", "shopify marketing automation"), expanded per theme.
  • Pricing: Google's own keyword data through the connected account (free on treg, and the most accurate), falling back to DataForSEO or SE Ranking. Low and high top-of-page bid ranges per country.
  • Estimated CPI per keyword uses that theme's own click-to-install history. A keyword stays out if even its low bid can't hit $25.
  • Negatives loaded before the first click, from the audit:
    • other platforms (WooCommerce, Wix, WordPress, BigCommerce);
    • login and sign-in terms;
    • android, iOS, chrome, disable, pop-up blocker;
    • bulk and mass SMS;
    • "shopify" on its own;
    • "age verification popup".
    That's about $2k of past waste. Your existing 2,048-term "EN Generic Negative KWs" list is reused.

Ads

  • 2 to 3 RSAs per ad group, 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, no pins. Pinning is what sank the 2023 popup ad.
  • Angles that won: "Email, Push, SMS in one", "Free unlimited active profiles", "Join 35,000+ stores", "Best email marketing for Shopify", abandoned-cart recovery. These come from the Test Lab's learned themes as well as history.
  • Guardrails: no competitor names in ad text (past ads said "Cheaper than Klaviyo" and "Klaviyo Email Marketing Rival"). Every claim goes through the claims ledger ("98% deliverability", "#1" and "50% off" need backing). One broken URL containing "??" was found and gets fixed.
  • Judged on installs, never ad strength.

The optimisation loop

RuleSetting
Keyword loserSpent 2.5x target ($50) with no installs, or under a 10% chance of beating $20: pause (the same maths as Meta). For broad match, try negatives first.
Keyword winner10+ installs, over 80% chance of beating $20: gets its own exact-match version and more budget share
Search termsMined daily. Terms with spend and no installs that match a negative pattern are proposed as negatives. Terms that convert are proposed as keywords. About 45% of spend never shows up in the search-term report, so keywords are judged too.
AdsMonthly: the weakest RSA in each ad group is replaced with a new one built from the best-performing assets and themes
Countries and deviceMonthly: a country over the $25 ceiling for 30 days is paused. Mobile comes back only if desktop has stayed under target for 30 days.
BudgetWeekly: shifts between campaigns toward the one under target, at most 20% per change
SecurityEvery sync: any new campaign, unknown final-URL domain, or budget over the cap raises a critical alert, so a repeat of Nov 2025 is caught in hours
