Box, Box, Brenda: split-screen thriller, 66 s

The race and Brenda's night play side by side. PushOwl chases her through push, email and SMS while Castell hunts Theo, and a WhatsApp discount wins her over as he takes the flag. 149 words of dialogue from six voices, all native to the video, plus a composed music layer and notification sound design.

Generation plan Four native generations, each set in one world; I composite them in the edit

R1: race part A, 30 s

From the hook to the pit entry. Dana is on camera for her first line and on the radio after. The action is composed in the middle band of the frame so the top half of the split screen crops cleanly.

BR: Brenda's night, 24 s

One continuous take in her apartment: the laptop flick ("Later."), the wine and the buzzing phone, the SMS ("Nope."), the blanket, then WhatsApp ("Ten percent? Fine.") and the tap to order. I cut her beats into the bottom half, each aligned to the race line it answers.

R2: race part B, 18 s

From the pit stop to the press room question: Marcus, the out lap and overtake, the flag, the podium.

E: the ending, 14 s

Both "PushOwl" lines plus the candle. Kept short because short generations pronounce the brand reliably. Three takes; I keep the one both transcribers hear as "PushOwl".

Sound, in four layers

1. Composed music layer

An original 66 s track (ElevenLabs Music) composed from a section plan matched to the timeline above. The style blends the second half of Chaos Theory (electronic chase build) with the energy of Action Sport Race (driving drums and guitar drive). It stings on each miss, drops at 25 to 30 s, slams at the pit stop, lifts on the flag, and carries through the outro. It ducks under every line.

2. Native race audio

Dialogue, engines, crowd and radio texture from R1, R2 and E. Full level in full-frame moments, about 6 dB down during split screens (dialogue lifted back up).

3. Brenda's room

Her native room tone, rain, wine and phone buzz, and her three muttered lines, low under the race, with her lines lifted so they're heard.

4. Graphics sound design

The only sound I add: push ping, swipe, email swoosh, SMS buzz, WhatsApp pop, tracker ticks, stamps, cash chime, each landing on its graphic's entrance.

Voice consistency The fix for Dana's voice changing

  • R1 is generated first. From the chosen take I cut a clean 3 to 5 s sample of each voice (Dana, Theo, the commentator, the spotter) and pass them into R2 and E as voice references, so the same people sound the same.
  • Delivery notes change energy, never register: "same voice, slightly faster", not "worried".
  • New automatic check: each character's lines are compared by voice fingerprint, and a take where a voice drifts is rejected, the same way a wrong word is.

Rules from the last cut

  • Objects keep one physical state per shot: phones never flip, and screen content only ever comes from our graphics.
  • No quotable words inside delivery notes. "No panic" was spoken aloud last time.
  • Graphics enter in 0.4 s or less and hold 1.5 s or less, in the free half of the split, never over a face.
  • 24 fps end to end (the model's native rate), so there's no judder.
  • Shot starts are measured frame by frame on each take, and every graphic and sound effect is anchored to its real cut or spoken word.
  • Brand words appear only in the short ending clip. The race lines are brand-free ("It's on autopilot").
