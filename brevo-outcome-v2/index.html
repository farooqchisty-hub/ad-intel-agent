1. The sound fix

I measured v1: while the narrator talks, the music sits only 1.7 dB below the voice, and 12 moments have an effect within 6 dB of it. That is why it felt like it was fighting him. The fix:

Music

  • 14 dB under the voice while it speaks, rising to 8 dB under in the gaps between lines, so it breathes with him.
  • Smooth dips on every spoken phrase (0.25 s in, 0.5 s out), never a hard duck.
  • Same quirky pizzicato and marimba track (it suits the tone), re-edited on its bar grid so the hits land on story beats: the drop out on "So stop buying a CRM", the groove back exactly on "Brevo" (v1 was 0.6 s late), and the track's real ending under the logo.
  • Two comic one-beat music stops, used as punchlines: after "everyone blames someone else" and after "nineteen times".

Sound effects

  • 77 cues down to about 22, one per meaningful event only.
  • Every cue at least 10 dB under the voice (levels 0.08 to 0.18), soft and short. No impacts, risers or whooshes on every cut.
  • Each cue is placed on the frame where its visual lands, which is itself placed on the word, so sound, picture and voice hit together.
  • AI clips play muted. QA checks the voice-to-music gap on every line before I send it.

2. The look: motion that shows off, with a wink

v1 was clean cards on a mint stage. v2 keeps the Brevo palette, type and real UI, but every scene gets a signature move, and the film carries a running deadpan joke: Jordan, head of ecommerce at Fernhill Home, literally wearing eight hats.

Camera, not slides

Transitions are camera moves with meaning: a zoom through the "o" of "who" into live action, an iris that shrinks Jordan's footage into the avatar, a pink hat thrown out of a graphic and caught in real footage.

Graphics glued to footage

AI shots are filmed locked off, so tags, leader lines and cards stay pinned to real objects (each hat, the folders, the phone) while the camera moves happen in the edit.

Real depth

3D card flips with a light sweep, UI panels fanning out in space, a bar that shoots out of the top of frame with the camera tilting up after it, paper that drops with weight and shadow.

Kinetic type as a character

"Stop buying a CRM." lands word by word on the beat, CRM gets a hand-drawn strike, and the pink Retention hat drops onto the word "retention" like it is wearing it.

The wink

Small deadpan asides, one at a time and never over a face: "Retention (part-time, Fridays, maybe)", a Blame-o-meter, "Tutorial 14 of 30", "The other 51%: very nice hangers", "(Yes, nineteen.)".

Footage rules

Natural daylight grade, no green glare or tint on any footage. Brand green lives only in graphics. No logos or readable text in the AI shots. All Brevo screens are HTML rebuilds of the real UI, never AI.

6. How it gets made

Generation

  • Seedance 2.5 (face model, through treg) at 1080p, each shot animated from the keyframe above, prompts written for capture: lens, light, one weighted action, real-time speed.
  • Total 42 s of footage, about $19.40. Keyframes so far: about $1.10.
  • One take each. A second take only for a real failure (broken hands or face, wrong physics, a logo appearing), then I finish the cut and flag anything minor for you.

Build and checks

  • Same HyperFrames project and voice; scenes rebuilt in parallel, then one render at 1080p 60 fps.
  • Checks before you see it: no snaps or dead air, every readable label held at least 1.2 s, voice at least 12 dB over the music on every line, -16 LUFS, no tint on any footage.
  • Still flagged from v1: the 19x claim is spoken and shown, and Will's brief says Everything Caravans' sign-off is pending.
