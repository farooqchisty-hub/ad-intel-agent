The 10 concepts

Y1The Storage Unit

N1 · paying for silent subscribersAI cinematic + walkthrough + motion graphicssuggested pick

The owner of a tea brand visits the storage facility where she has been renting one locker for every subscriber on her list, and learns that the Black Friday signups have just added three thousand more lockers to next month's rent, whether or not anyone ever opens them.

Genre
Deadpan comedy, one world, 60 s
First 5 s
A slow dolly down an endless fluorescent corridor of identical locker doors. Dana, on camera: "I pay rent every month on twelve thousand people who never visit."
Cast, places
Dana, 41, owner of Fernwood Tea Co., cardigan, practical. Gil, 60s, the facility manager, clipboard, perfectly calm. A self-storage corridor and the front office.
How it proves the product
Lockers are contacts and rent is per-contact pricing. Gil opens a locker: dust, and a tag that reads "Last opened: never". The turn: Dana asks whether anywhere charges only when she actually ships something, and Gil says, "That would be postage." The walkthrough then shows the real PushOwl contacts page (unlimited) and billing (charged per email sent).
Beats
  1. Hook, the endless corridor
  2. Setup: Gil explains the monthly rent per locker
  3. Turn: the "never opened" locker, then three thousand new Black Friday lockers
  4. Build: "That would be postage", and the switch to how PushOwl bills
  5. Payoff: rent counter against postage counter in motion graphics
  6. Close: Dana states the offer while walking out
Why landscape
The endless corridor is a horizontal image. At 16:9 the scale of the list is the joke.
30 s cut
Hook, the "never opened" locker, the postage line, close.
Evidence
"Paying for ghost subscribers" is the angle that held viewers best in the live account (41% view rate). This finally shows the idea instead of saying it.
Risks
Signage must stay blank (no real storage brands). The locker count is a picture, not a claim.
Cost
Storyboard about $5 · production about $45

Y2Black Friday: Hard Mode

N1 · paying for silent subscribersAnimation + motion graphics + walkthrough

A side-scrolling video game. A store-owner hero runs through the Black Friday level collecting subscriber coins, but every coin makes the boss at the end, The Monthly Bill, grow bigger. Then she grabs the PushOwl power-up, and collecting becomes free.

Genre
Retro platformer animation, comedy, 60 s
First 5 s
Pixel title card "BLACK FRIDAY · HARD MODE", the hero leaps for a coin and the boss's health bar grows. Narrator: "With per-contact pricing, every subscriber you collect makes the bill bigger."
Cast, places
An original pixel-art hero (store owner with an apron), grey "ghost coins" (silent subscribers), the boss, and a strategist character who hands over a map. Levels: storefront, warehouse, checkout.
How it proves the product
The game rule is the pricing model. Collecting is normally good, but here it feeds the boss, and even grey coins that never open count. After the power-up, the heads-up display switches from "per contact" to "per send" and the boss shrinks. A bonus level shows the free extras: a strategist (free session), a moving cart (free migration), a treasure chest of ads (one-click ads plus $200 in credits). The final screen sits inside a pixel monitor showing the real PushOwl contacts page.
Beats
  1. Hook, title card and coin
  2. Setup: the Black Friday coin rain, the boss swells
  3. Turn: ghost coins still feed it
  4. Build: the power-up, the boss shrinks
  5. Payoff: the bonus level with the extras
  6. Close: "Continue?" screen with the real deadline
Why landscape
Side-scrollers are horizontal by nature, and the level reads left to right like time.
30 s cut
Hook, ghost coins, power-up, close.
Evidence
N1 is the strongest-holding angle in the account. Game framing stands out in a feed of talking heads.
Risks
Sprites must be original and look nothing like famous game characters. Text in pixel fonts must stay readable on a TV.
Cost
About $15 (sprite art, narrator voice, music); animation in our motion-graphics kit

Y3Two Stores, One Owner

N2 · what you'd saveAI cinematic split screen + motion graphicssuggested pick

A split screen down the middle. The same owner, played by one actor, runs the same Black Friday on both sides: same products, same list, same number of emails. The only difference is that the left side's tool bills every contact and the right side's bills only what she sends. On December 1st, both versions open the bill.

Genre
Split-screen comedy drama, two mirrored worlds, 60 s
First 5 s
Two identical workshops, two identical owners, perfectly in sync: "We have the same store, the same list and the same Black Friday."
Cast, places
Rosa, 38, owner of Harbor & Hide (leather goods), apron, rolled sleeves; the same wardrobe on both sides. A leather workshop, duplicated.
How it proves the product
Everything matches until the bill. A contact counter rises identically on both sides (15,000 to 20,000). On 1 December the left Rosa opens about $375 and the right Rosa opens $37.50, with the footnote on screen. The right Rosa explains why in one calm sentence: "I pay for the emails I send, not for the people on my list, so a bigger list doesn't make my bill bigger."
Beats
  1. Hook, the sync
  2. Setup: Black Friday orders and signups on both sides
  3. Turn: 1 December, the two invoices
  4. Build: the explanation
  5. Payoff: the divider slides away and left Rosa walks over to the right side
  6. Close: right Rosa states the offer
Why landscape
Two full-height worlds side by side only work at 16:9.
30 s cut
Hook, the two invoices, the explanation, close.
Evidence
N2 is approved with footnoted multiples. Split screen is the most landscape-native device there is.
Risks
The same face, voice and wardrobe across two generations (identity cards plus a wardrobe lock). The in-sync line across two takes needs careful timing. The footnote must be readable on a TV.
Cost
Storyboard about $5 · production about $65 (two worlds)

Y4Found Money

N2 · what you'd saveAI cinematic ensemble + motion graphics + walkthrough

A three-person store team sits down to plan their Black Friday budget. The junior on the team works out what their email would cost on send-based pricing and finds about four thousand dollars a year, and the real argument begins: what to do with it.

Genre
Workplace ensemble comedy, one world, 60 s
First 5 s
A cramped office, whiteboard covered in Black Friday plans. Theo, the junior, looks up from his laptop: "We just found about four thousand dollars a year in our email bill."
Cast, places
Mara, 45, founder of Bramble Pet Supply; Theo, 24, ops; June, 33, marketing. Their small office above the stockroom.
How it proves the product
Theo explains the maths in plain sentences while a motion-graphics overlay builds it: 20,000 contacts, 2 emails each a month, about $375 a month with per-contact pricing against $37.50, about $4,050 a year (footnoted). The walkthrough shows where he found it: the contacts and billing screens. The team's ideas (more stock, more ads, a holiday bonus) lead into the extras: free migration, a free strategy session, ads in one click plus $200 in credits.
Beats
  1. Hook, the found money
  2. Setup: Mara doesn't believe it
  3. Turn: Theo walks them through the maths
  4. Build: the three of them argue about how to spend it
  5. Payoff: June notes the move and the setup are free too
  6. Close: Mara decides, and states the offer
Why landscape
Three people around a table and a whiteboard need a wide frame to play reactions against each other.
30 s cut
Hook, the maths, Mara's decision, close.
Evidence
N2's yearly-saving framing ("contacts in, yearly saving out") turned into a story.
Risks
Three faces and voices in one world (identity cards for all three). Overlapping dialogue must stay intelligible.
Cost
Storyboard about $5 · production about $50

Y5The Strategy Session

N3 · done for youWalkthrough-led + AI webcam tiles + motion graphics

We sit in on a 60-second compression of the free strategy session. A PushOwl strategist and a swimwear store owner share a screen, and in the time it takes to finish a coffee the strategist sets up her welcome pop-up, her cart reminders across push, email and SMS, and her Black Friday campaign, explaining each choice in plain language.

Genre
Screen-share documentary, 60 s
First 5 s
A video-call layout: the PushOwl dashboard fills the frame, with two webcam tiles. The strategist: "Before your biggest week, we'll set up your flows together, for free."
Cast, places
Nadia, 36, PushOwl strategist, headset, bright home office. Cole, 42, owner of Tidewater Swim, at his kitchen table.
How it proves the product
The product is the picture. Real screen recordings with zoom on each click and the rest of the screen dimmed, in three chapters marked on screen: 1 Pop-up, 2 Cart reminders, 3 Black Friday campaign. Cole's line at the end: "That's done?" Nadia: "That's done, and on Black Friday it runs on its own."
Beats
  1. Hook, the call opens
  2. Setup: Cole admits he hasn't touched his flows since spring
  3. Turn: "Let's start with the shoppers who leave without buying."
  4. Build: the three chapters
  5. Payoff: "That's done?"
  6. Close: Nadia states the offer, the session's $300 value on screen only
Why landscape
A screen share is a 16:9 world, and it looks like the real thing because it is the real interface.
30 s cut
Hook, chapter 2 (cart reminders), "That's done?", close.
Evidence
Human help is the most durable BFCM angle in the research. This is the only concept where the product is the whole picture.
Risks
Needs the demo store. "Dramatization" label (an AI person plays a PushOwl employee). No promised results; SMS is sold separately, so never imply free SMS.
Cost
Storyboard about $5 · production about $60 (two talking tiles)

Y6The Busiest Night

N3 · done for youAI cinematic split screen + walkthrough + motion graphicssuggested pick

Black Friday, 9:12 pm, in real time across a wide split screen. On the left, a shopper puts a jacket in her cart and gets pulled away. On the right, the store's owner switches off the warehouse lights after a long day. A clock runs across the top while the flows her strategist set up do the follow-up, and at 8:03 the next morning she wakes up to the order.

Genre
Real-time thriller with a warm ending, two worlds, 60 s
First 5 s
A clock across the top, "9:12 PM · BLACK FRIDAY". Left: the shopper closes her laptop on a full cart. Right: the owner at the light switch, on camera: "On Black Friday night, I can't chase every shopper who leaves without buying."
Cast, places
Erin, 47, owner of Wildroot Outfitters, in her warehouse, then at home. Lauren, 33, the shopper, on a couch with a phone call pulling her away.
How it proves the product
The cart reminder sequence plays out as events. A browser notification lands on Lauren's laptop (with a generic, unbranded operating-system look), then an email with the jacket, then a text the next morning, and she buys. A picture-in-picture shows the real cart-recovery flow with the same three steps, and the clock and timeline nodes are motion graphics.
Beats
  1. Hook, the clock and the line
  2. Setup: the abandoned cart
  3. Turn: the first reminder lands while Erin sleeps
  4. Build: the email, then the text the next morning
  5. Payoff: Erin's phone lights up with the order
  6. Close: Erin says the flows were set up for her for free, then the offer
Why landscape
Two worlds that affect each other side by side, with a timeline running across the full width.
30 s cut
Hook, the reminder, the order, close.
Evidence
Cart recovery is PushOwl's core job, and the cart-recovery headlines had the best click rates in the remarketing campaigns. This shows the done-for-you session paying off on the night it matters.
Risks
Reminder timings must match the app's template defaults (verify) or be labelled "Example". No recovery-rate numbers. Notification screens stay unbranded. "Dramatization" label.
Cost
Storyboard about $5 · production about $65 (two worlds)

Y7Opening Night

N4 · ads in one clickAI cinematic + walkthrough picture-in-picture + motion graphics

On the eve of Black Friday, a coffee roaster walks into a small gallery opening where every framed piece on the wall is a Black Friday ad for her own store. The gallerist explains they were made from nothing but her store's link, which she pasted on her phone in the cab outside a minute earlier.

Genre
Light magic realism, one world, 60 s
First 5 s
A slow, wide track along a white gallery wall of framed ads. Ava, stepping in: "Every ad on this wall was made from my store's link."
Cast, places
Ava, 34, owner of Copper Kettle Coffee. Simon, 50s, the gallerist, dry and precise. Shoppers as gallery guests. A small white-walled gallery at night.
How it proves the product
A picture-in-picture flashback shows the real feature: she pastes her link and the ads appear. The framed ads on the wall are real outputs for a fictional store in USD, each tagged "Example". She picks four ("Those four go live tomorrow"), the frames get "LIVE" tags, and motion graphics add "$200 in ad credits included".
Beats
  1. Hook, the wall
  2. Setup: "All we needed was your link."
  3. Turn: the cab flashback
  4. Build: guests stop at her ads
  5. Payoff: four go live
  6. Close: Ava states the offer
Why landscape
A gallery wall is a horizontal pan, and eight frames read at once.
30 s cut
Hook, the flashback, four go live, close.
Evidence
N4 hook; the ads feature is available to PushOwl users.
Risks
AI video garbles text inside frames, so the real ads are composited onto the frames in post, which needs locked-off or slow tracked shots. Needs a USD example ad set.
Cost
Storyboard about $5 · production about $50 plus compositing

Y8The Oner

N4 · ads in one clickPure walkthrough + motion graphics, no AIsuggested pick

One unbroken screen recording with a real clock in the corner. We paste a store's link into PushOwl, watch its Black Friday ads appear, choose four and get them ready to launch. A calm narrator explains each step as it happens, with no cuts to hide anything.

Genre
Real-time product proof, 60 s
First 5 s
A stopwatch starts at 00:00 over the PushOwl dashboard. Narrator: "This is one unbroken recording, so you can see exactly how long it takes."
Cast, places
No people. The PushOwl app on the demo store, a narrator voice.
How it proves the product
The clock is the proof. Zoom on each click, the rest of the screen dimmed, and short labels for each step. If generation takes longer than the video allows, a visible "sped up 4x" label stays on screen, so it stays honest. It ends on the four chosen ads, "$200 in ad credits included" and the offer.
Beats
  1. Hook, the stopwatch
  2. Setup: paste the link
  3. Turn: the ads appear
  4. Build: choose four
  5. Payoff: the clock stops
  6. Close: the offer, read over the end card
Why landscape
Desktop software is 16:9 already; nothing gets cropped or shrunk.
30 s cut
Stopwatch, ads appear, clock stops, close.
Evidence
The teardown found that no live ad shows the product being used. This is the purest fix and the cheapest test.
Risks
Needs the demo store, and the ads must look good on the first run because it's one take.
Cost
About $5 (narrator voice, music)

Y9Final Boarding Call

Full offer + deadlineMotion graphics + walkthrough inserts

An airport departure board in the Brevo flat style flips through the Black Friday offer like flights. Each row is one part of the deal, each with a gate that closes on 30 November at 11:59 pm Pacific, and a calm gate announcer explains what each one gives you. In the last week, the top row flips to "Final call".

Genre
Motion-graphics announcement, 60 s
First 5 s
The split-flap board clatters into "BLACK FRIDAY · BOARDING". Announcer: "Boarding now: PushOwl's Black Friday offer for new and free-plan stores."
Cast, places
A narrator voice and the board. Short walkthrough inserts open from each row (for example, the migration row opens onto the contact import screen).
How it proves the product
Each row flips, then opens into a two-second real-interface insert that shows what that part of the deal is. Rows: 50% off annual, 30% off monthly, free migration, free strategy session, Black Friday ads in one click, $200 in ad credits.
Beats
  1. Hook, the board
  2. Rows 1 and 2: the discount
  3. Rows 3 and 4: done for you
  4. Rows 5 and 6: the ads
  5. The gate time flips to the deadline
  6. Close: "Final call" version for the last week
Why landscape
Departure boards are wide; six rows and a gate column fill 16:9 perfectly.
30 s cut
Hook, rows 1, 3 and 5, the deadline.
Evidence
Built for retargeting people who visited the BFCM page and for the final week, when the deadline is the message.
Risks
The discount rows are slots until the offer is final. Sound on each row flip has to be justified (one soft flap per row, listed for approval).
Cost
About $5 (narrator voice, music)

Y10The Forecast

N3 · done for you, with urgencyAI presenter + motion-graphics map + walkthrough

A local-TV weather presenter stands in front of a map of Thanksgiving week and gives the Black Friday forecast for online store owners: heavy traffic arriving Friday, scattered abandoned carts through the weekend, and a cold front of list growth that reaches per-contact email bills in December. Her "be prepared" segment shows how to get ready this week, with the setup done for free.

Genre
Weather-report parody, one world, 60 s
First 5 s
A wide studio shot, a lower third "BLACK FRIDAY FORECAST · ONLINE STORES". The presenter: "Good evening, store owners. The Black Friday storm arrives this Friday."
Cast, places
Kim, 50, veteran weather presenter, perfectly earnest. A TV studio with a blank screen wall; the map is motion graphics composited behind her.
How it proves the product
The map plays the season: a traffic front sweeping in, cart icons scattering, a December cold front labelled "per-contact bills". The "be prepared" segment cuts to an inset box of real walkthrough: cart reminders, the Black Friday campaign, and the free setup session.
Beats
  1. Hook, the forecast
  2. Setup: the traffic front
  3. Turn: scattered carts
  4. Build: the December cold front
  5. Payoff: the "be prepared" segment
  6. Close: "The window closes Monday night, November thirtieth", then the offer
Why landscape
TV weather is a 16:9 format, and the map needs the width.
30 s cut
Hook, the cold front, "be prepared", close.
Evidence
Timed for the warm-up and launch phases, when owners are still preparing. Leads with done-for-you, and touches on list cost too.
Risks
No forecast numbers (they would read as fake statistics). Her gestures must match the map, so the map is built to her pointing after the take. No real cities.
Cost
Storyboard about $5 · production about $45 plus compositing
