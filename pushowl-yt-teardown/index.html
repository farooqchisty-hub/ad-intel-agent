PushOwl YouTube ads: creative teardown and a better video system

15 Oct 2026 · Google Ads 846-525-4800 · the 4 live Demand Gen campaigns (YT ROW, YT India and their 2 remarketing twins) · 22 enabled ads, 8 unique videos · delivery data 1 Apr to 14 Oct 2026 (per-video asset report) · frames are YouTube's 1-per-second scrub previews.

In short
  • Google spent 77% of the video budget on two old landscape brand films (2023 and 2024) that pitch web push, while every headline and the landing page sell pay-per-send email ("$25 for 10K emails"). The viewer is promised one product and shown another.
  • The vertical creator videos hold attention 4 to 6 times better (24 to 41% view rate against 6 to 7%), but they get little budget because each ad also carries a brand film and Demand Gen keeps choosing it.
  • All six vertical videos are one template: a talking head under a static screenshot, the same recycled dashboard footage, the same blue price card, and pricing tables too small to read on a phone. Nobody ever uses the product on screen.
  • Things on screen that must not ship again: a dashboard banner that says "Introducing MagicFit AI agents" (another product), a competitor named on the price card, "Shopify" in the voiceover, and a BFCM 2025 video (14-day trial, stock "80% OFF" art) still live in October 2026.
  • Best idea to build on: "paying for ghost subscribers". Its video kept 41% of viewers and 45% watched to the end on YT ROW.

What to make instead: one hybrid video system

Every new video stacks four layers. Each layer has one job, so nothing is decoration.

1. AI presenter (story)A North American store owner on camera in a real setting, speaking in calm, complete sentences. Generated with native in-video audio, about 30 s single takes, through the cinematic-ads-harness. Carries the hook and the emotion.
2. Real product walkthrough (proof)Screen recordings of the actual PushOwl app on a demo store in USD: auto zoom on the click that matters, smoothed cursor, the rest of the screen dimmed. Shown as picture-in-picture or full-screen inserts while the presenter keeps talking.
3. Motion graphics (explain)The mechanism the UI can't show: two invoices printing side by side, a per-contact bill climbing while the per-send bill stays flat, a cart-recovery timeline (abandon, push at 30 min, email at 2 h, order). Built in HyperFrames with our existing receipt, counter and kinetic-type parts.
4. Animation (metaphor)Short character beats that make the idea memorable: ghost subscribers drifting through a contact list, each one dropping a coin into the bill. Used for 2 to 4 s at a time, never over a face.

Rules every new video follows

  • Spoken lines say "PushOwl"; visuals may show the Brevo PushOwl lockup. Never "Shopify" or a competitor's name, spoken or on screen; the audience is "online store owners".
  • Calm, explanatory narration (about 130 to 145 words a minute), complete sentences, numbers with their conditions.
  • Full videos 60 s, cutdowns 30 s, nothing shorter.
  • Delivered in 9:16, 16:9 and 1:1 so Demand Gen has the right shape for every placement; text inside the Shorts safe zone; anything readable stays on screen long enough to read and big enough for a phone.
  • Product footage comes from a demo store with USD prices and fictional data, never from a real merchant's account, and never from a frame that shows another product.
  • Every claim on screen is current and sourced (no 2022 stats, no expired offers).

Account fixes so the better videos get the budget

  • Remove the two brand films and the BFCM 2025 video from the live ads. Demand Gen will keep picking the cheapest-to-serve asset as long as it is there.
  • One concept per ad, with headlines and landing page written for that concept, so the click lands on the promise the video made.
  • Judge new videos on hook rate (25% watched) and hold (100% watched) in week one, then on installs once the tracking fixes are live.
