Brevo: five cinematic video concepts

Stage 1 of the cinematic video harness (the system behind "Box, Box, Brenda"). Product: Brevo for ecommerce brands. Written 7 Oct 2026 from the 4 Oct library sweep (14 video briefs) and the Brevo claims ledger. Pick 3 or 4 and they go to storyboards. Nothing is generated before you approve a storyboard.

How to pick
  • Reply with the IDs you want storyboarded (e.g. "C1, C3, C4"), plus any changes to a concept. Your edits override the card.
  • Every number on screen comes from the verified Brevo ledger (seed/claims.sql). Claims marked placeholder or blocked (migration time, inbox placement rate, merchant receipts) are not used.
  • Runtimes are 35 to 50 s, 9:16, cast for North America. Each concept is one cinematic world built from 3 to 5 generations, with one composed score.
Claims these concepts draw on (all verified)
  • Send-based: Brevo Starter and Business bill on monthly email volume, not on how many contacts you store. Starter is $29 a month for 40,000 emails, $65 for 100,000.
  • List tax: a profile-based platform bills on every contact who could be emailed. 10,000 contacts sending 40,000 emails: $150 vs Brevo $29. 50,000 contacts sending 100,000: $720 vs $65. A BFCM list surge raises next month's bill on profile-based billing; on send-based billing it does not.
  • List truths: silent subscribers are 20 to 40% of a promotional ecommerce list after 90 days; lists decay about 22% a year.
  • Services and features: Brevo managed services runs retention for merchants without a retention team and handles the migration; Brevo generates ad campaigns and UGC-style videos from products already synced from your store; Brevo shows you the competitor ads worth beating.

C1Dark comedy / night-shift thriller45 sNarrative: list tax (N1)

The Meter

On BFCM night a candle-shop owner's email bill turns into a taxi meter bolted to her desk, ticking up with every new signup, including the ones who will never open a thing. She grabs the meter, walks it out to the curb and hails a different ride: one that only charges when it moves.

First 3 s: Extreme close-up: a chrome taxi meter on a cluttered packing desk, its drum clicking over with a sharp mechanical tick as a phone beside it buzzes. Line (her, on camera, deadpan): "Why is my email bill running like a cab?"
Cast
Jess, 34, owner of a small candle brand, flannel over a black tee, hair in a claw clip, tired but funny. A rideshare-style driver for the button (40s, warm, says one line).
Locations
Her garage-turned-warehouse at night (shelves of jars, packing tape, string lights); the curb outside under a streetlamp.
Product mechanic and proof
The meter is the profile-based bill: it ticks on every contact stored, not on sends. The story proves it by having it tick while she isn't sending anything (she's eating cereal; tick, tick). The turn: the Brevo ride's meter stays still while she sits, and only moves when she hits send. Graphic cards: "10,000 contacts, 40,000 emails: $150 vs $29" and "Bills on emails sent, not contacts stored".
Beats
  1. Hook: the meter ticks, her line.
  2. Setup: BFCM signups pour in (phone buzzing); every buzz ticks the meter. She's not even sending.
  3. Turn: a signup notification reads like a ghost (never opened since spring); tick. She unscrews the meter.
  4. Build: she carries the meter out to the curb like a bad roommate, sets it on the bin.
  5. Payoff: a car pulls up; the driver: "We charge when you go somewhere." Comparison card.
  6. In-world CTA: she gets in: "Brevo. Bill me for the sends."
  7. Outro: Brevo end card.
Devices
Single world, so plain cutting, no split screen. The meter is a real prop with a blank display; the digits are built in the edit so the numbers stay exact. Added sound: the meter tick (the audience must register "it charges me"), carried by the native take where possible.
Generation units
One 25 s continuous warehouse take (brand-free), one 10 s curb take, one 5 s insert for the brand line in the car (exact-risk unit).
Evidence
Library briefs 82abdcbe "The platform bill follows the list", 6e6160fc "The list surge is not the bill surge"; claims klaviyo-active-profile, brevo-send-based, listtax-10k, bfcm-bill-spike.
Est. cost
About $35 to $55 through final (3 generations, 2 to 3 score sections, typical re-rolls); storyboard about $3.
Risks
  • Taxi meter digits must be built in the edit; generated displays smear numbers.
  • The comparison names a profile-based competitor. On screen we say "profile-based platform"; naming Klaviyo is a legal and brand call for you.
  • Physics: the meter is bolted, then unscrewed; one physical state per shot.
C2Horror-comedy40 sNarrative: silent subscribers (N1)

Ghost List

A boutique owner locking up after a sale finds her shop full of pale, silent shoppers who never buy, never open, never leave, and each one is on her monthly bill. Her assistant whispers the line every horror film needs: they've been here for 90 days.

First 3 s: The shop lights flicker; a row of motionless customers stands in the aisle, faces lit by a dead phone glow, all staring at nothing. Line (assistant, whisper): "Rae... they don't buy. They never buy."
Cast
Rae, 41, owner of an apparel boutique, cropped grey blazer, silver hoops, unflappable. Tomás, 25, her assistant, hoodie and lanyard, scared. Six to eight "silent subscribers": ordinary shoppers with a cold grade and a faint translucency, all different looks.
Locations
A small apparel boutique after hours (racks, a counter, a neon "open" sign switching off).
Product mechanic and proof
Silent subscribers are 20 to 40% of a promotional list after 90 days, and profile-based billing charges for every one. The turn: Rae doesn't exorcise them. She shrugs: on send-based billing they can stay, because Brevo bills on emails sent, not contacts stored. She sends only to the living, the ghosts fade into the background, a real customer walks in.
Beats
  1. Hook: flicker, the silent crowd, Tomás's whisper.
  2. Setup: a ghost's tag reads "subscribed: June, opened: never". Card: "20 to 40% of a promo list goes silent in 90 days".
  3. Turn: Tomás: "And we pay for every one of them." Rae, calm: "Not anymore."
  4. Build: she flips the sign back to open and sends to the people who actually open.
  5. Payoff: the ghosts stay put but fade; a real customer pushes the door, bell rings. Card: "Brevo bills on emails sent, not contacts stored".
  6. In-world CTA: Rae to camera: "Keep your list. Pay for your sends. Brevo."
  7. Outro.
Devices
Single world, plain cutting. The horror language (flicker, held silence, whisper) is the device; the score stays near-silent until the door bell. Added sounds: the door bell (a real customer, the payoff event) only.
Generation units
One 25 s continuous shop take with the crowd, one 10 s turn take, one 5 s CTA unit (brand line).
Evidence
Library brief 82abdcbe; claims silent-list, klaviyo-active-profile, brevo-send-based.
Est. cost
About $30 to $50 through final; storyboard about $3.
Risks
  • Crowd consistency across takes (8 faces); keep the crowd in one long take and use inserts for close-ups.
  • Translucency must read as stylized, not glitchy: test in the storyboard stills first.
  • Tone: horror must stay light; no jump-scare sound on a brand line.

The People v. Paula Reyes

A sneaker-store owner stands trial for the crime of growing her email list over Black Friday. The prosecution reads her next month's bill like a rap sheet. Her public defender, unbothered, asks one question: "Your Honor, what did she actually send?"

First 3 s: A gavel slams; the prosecutor turns to the jury holding an invoice. Line (prosecutor, booming): "The defendant grew her list by fifty thousand people."
Cast
Paula, 38, owner of a sneaker shop, varsity jacket, gold chain, defiant. Prosecutor, 55, three-piece suit, theatrical. Defender, 30s, rumpled cardigan, sleepy confidence. Judge, 60s. A jury of shop-owner types.
Locations
A wood-panelled courtroom, slightly heightened like a TV legal drama.
Product mechanic and proof
The bill is the evidence: profile-based billing charges for every contact who could be emailed, so the BFCM surge raises next month's bill. The defence's exhibit: send-based billing. Exhibit cards: "50,000 contacts, 100,000 emails: $720 vs $65" and "A BFCM list surge doesn't raise a send-based bill". Verdict: not guilty.
Beats
  1. Hook: gavel, the charge.
  2. Setup: the prosecutor reads the bill ("seven hundred and twenty dollars"); the jury gasps.
  3. Turn: the defender stands: "What did she actually send?" Paula: "A hundred thousand emails."
  4. Build: Exhibit B on the courtroom screen, the comparison card.
  5. Payoff: the jury foreperson: "Not guilty." Paula exhales.
  6. In-world CTA: on the courthouse steps, a reporter's mic; Paula: "Grow the list. Pay for the sends. Brevo."
  7. Outro.
Devices
Single world plus a short epilogue; courtroom coverage (singles, reverse shots) is the device. Numbers spoken on camera go in short units (exact risk). Added sounds: the gavel (twice: charge and verdict) because the audience must register both.
Generation units
One 25 s courtroom take (charge and bill, brand-free), one 10 s defence-and-verdict take, one 5 s exhibit insert, one 5 s steps CTA unit.
Evidence
Library briefs 3262300c "The peak-week bill check", 2fc6b238 "Ask the pricing-model question", 6e6160fc; claims listtax-50k, bfcm-bill-spike, brevo-send-based, brevo-tier-100k.
Est. cost
About $45 to $70 through final (4 units, more speaking parts); storyboard about $4.
Risks
  • Five speaking roles: voice consistency needs voice references in every unit.
  • Spoken numbers ("seven hundred and twenty") are re-roll prone; they sit in short units.
  • Comparative claim: same naming decision as C1.
  • Timely for BFCM: if picked, it should ship before mid-November.
C4Heist / caper45 sNarrative: managed services (N4)

Overnight Crew

A one-person skincare brand wants to switch platforms but can't afford a week offline. At midnight a quiet, impeccably coordinated crew arrives and moves her entire email program, flows, segments, templates, as glowing crates into a new building across the street. By sunrise her welcome email is already going out from the new place.

First 3 s: A crew leader in a dark Brevo-green jacket checks a watch under a streetlight; behind him four movers lift a crate labelled "ABANDONED CART FLOW". Line (crew leader, low): "In by midnight. Out by morning."
Cast
Nina, 29, founder of a skincare brand, oversized sweatshirt, glasses, anxious planner. Marcus, 50s, crew leader, calm, salt-and-pepper beard. Four movers of different ages and looks.
Locations
Two facing buildings on a quiet street at night (her old cramped office, a bright new space); her bedroom at dawn for the epilogue.
Product mechanic and proof
Brevo managed services handles the migration and runs retention for merchants without a retention team. The crates are her real assets made physical (labelled with flow names); the proof is the dawn shot: her phone shows the welcome email going out from the new setup. No migration-time number is used (that claim is a placeholder).
Beats
  1. Hook: the watch, the line, the crate.
  2. Setup: Nina at her window, worried: "I can't stop selling to switch."
  3. Turn: Marcus: "You don't stop. We move."
  4. Build: the heist montage: crates carried across, a segment crate handed hand to hand, a template crate slid into place.
  5. Payoff: dawn, Nina wakes; a real order email preview (built in the edit from real Brevo UI) lands.
  6. In-world CTA: Marcus at the door, tipping two fingers: "Brevo managed services. We'll take it from here."
  7. Outro.
Devices
Two places but one storyline, so plain cutting; the heist montage is the device (match cuts on crates). Added sound: the email arriving at dawn (the payoff event). The crate moves stay silent.
Generation units
One 25 s night street take (crew and crates, brand-free), one 10 s window and dialogue take, one 5 s dawn insert, one 5 s CTA unit (brand line, "managed services").
Evidence
Library briefs 0c09d3a5 "Switching platforms should not become a project", 2aa0d027 "Retention, handled"; claim managed-services.
Est. cost
About $40 to $60 through final; storyboard about $3.
Risks
  • Crate labels are text: keep them as blank crates in generation and add labels in the edit, or accept re-rolls.
  • Managed services is a specific offering: confirm it's available for the target segment before launch.
  • Night exteriors need strict physics (lifting heavy crates, doors).

The Dossier

A coffee-roaster founder is handed a classified dossier: every ad her biggest rival is running, the ones worth beating flagged in red. Her handler slides across a second folder: her own counter-campaign, already built from the products in her store.

First 3 s: A manila folder slaps onto a diner table; it falls open to glossy ad printouts with red circles. Line (handler, quiet): "These are the ones you need to beat."
Cast
Dee, 45, founder of a coffee roaster, denim apron over a white shirt, reading glasses on her head, sharp. The handler, 60s, trench coat, unhurried.
Locations
A late-night diner booth (neon, rain on the window); her roastery at dawn for the epilogue.
Product mechanic and proof
Brevo shows you the competitor ads worth beating, and generates ad campaigns and UGC-style videos from the products already synced from your store. Folder one is the intel (built from real Brevo screens in the edit, rival ads fictional); folder two is her own campaign, made from her actual coffee bags (product shots from the catalog).
Beats
  1. Hook: the folder lands, the line.
  2. Setup: Dee flips through, rival ads circled: "They're spending all of this?"
  3. Turn: the handler slides folder two: "Yours. From what's already on your shelves."
  4. Build: real Brevo UI insert: synced products becoming ad creatives.
  5. Payoff: dawn at the roastery, her new ad running on a phone propped by the grinder.
  6. In-world CTA: Dee closes the dossier: "Brevo. Know what you're up against."
  7. Outro.
Devices
Single storyline, plain cutting; the dossier is the device. Product visuals only as real Brevo UI inserts. Added sound: none beyond native diner ambience and rain; the score carries the tension.
Generation units
One 20 s diner take, one 10 s roastery dawn take, one 5 s CTA unit.
Evidence
Library briefs 6e14e5a3 "See the work, then see what is worth beating", 7717a704, 154a5673, b13f8c28 (catalog to campaign); claims competitor-spy, magicfit-in-app.
Est. cost
About $30 to $45 through final; storyboard about $3.
Risks
  • Rival ads must be fictional brands on supplied boards (real-brand leaks are likely in print props).
  • Needs a screen recording of the in-app ad generation and competitor view; confirm which Brevo UI is approved to show.
