01The thesis

The instinct is to build an ad generator with a competitor feed bolted on. That is the wrong centre of gravity, because generation is the part we already solved. There is a working static pipeline that has shipped 247 ads through a ranked template library with brand safety, chroma compositing and a vision QA gate. Adding another image model call is not the hard part.

The hard part is judgment. Ad libraries are a firehose of creative with no performance data attached. Meta publishes no spend, no impressions and no CTR for commercial ads. Google publishes only where and how long. So an agent that "looks at competitor ads and makes similar ones" is copying noise: most of what Klaviyo is running is losing, and we cannot see which half.

The agent's real job is to infer which competitor ads are winning from public signals, decompose those winners into arguments rather than pictures, and then run our own arguments against our own performance data so the loop compounds.

Three consequences follow, and they should drive every build decision:

  1. The unit of intelligence is the angle, not the image. "Klaviyo added seven ads on the deliverability angle this week and retired the AI agents angle" is an insight. "Here are forty new JPEGs" is a folder.
  2. The unit of value is the join. Every ad we generate carries a pointer to the competitor angle it descended from. Once that ad runs, its real CTR and CPA flow back to the angle. After a quarter we can say which competitor arguments actually work for our audience. Nobody can buy that. It is the only reason to build instead of paying Atria.
  3. Copying is a validated hypothesis, not a shortcut. Klaviyo sells to merchants who already have a list and a budget. Brevo ecom has a top-of-funnel problem. An angle that converts for them can flop for us, so every adapted angle goes in as a test with a kill rule, not as a truth.

03Four hard truths that shape the design

1. Ad libraries carry no performance data, so longevity is the only honest proxy

Meta gives an ad's start date and whether it is still active. Google gives firstShown, lastShown and shownForDays. That is the whole signal set. An advertiser with a competent media buyer kills losers inside 7 to 14 days, so an ad that has been live for 60 days is almost certainly paying for itself. Everything else we can infer, we infer from behaviour: how many variants they cut of one concept, whether they re-launched something they killed, how many regions they expanded it to. Build the scoring on those, and label it honestly in the UI as an inference, not a metric.

2. The library undercounts and mislabels, systematically

Google's own number_of_ads_estimate is rounded and overstates. Regions listed are where impressions were recorded, not where they targeted, and anything under roughly a thousand impressions is suppressed entirely. Meta keyword search returns other advertisers' ads, so results must always be filtered by page id. One brand routinely has several legal entities with separate advertiser ids, so a domain sweep that finds one entity is an incomplete sweep. Each of these is a silent data-quality bug that will quietly corrupt week-over-week deltas, so each needs an explicit guard in the ingest layer, not a comment in a README.

3. Meta has no real draft object, Google does

There is no "draft ad" in the Marketing API. The pattern that works is a real campaign created with status: PAUSED all the way down the tree, in a reserved campaign whose name marks it as agent output, so a human can open Ads Manager, preview the rendered ad, and flip it live. Google Ads is different: it has genuine CampaignDraft objects that stage changes against a base campaign, and it now does real-time policy review on responsive search ads, which means we can get a policy verdict before a human ever looks. Design the delivery layer per platform rather than pretending they are symmetric.

4. Naming a competitor in an ad is a legal decision, not a creative one

Our sharpest wedge is that Klaviyo charges for profiles you are not emailing. That is also the claim most likely to attract a letter. The agent must be able to generate the angle without naming the competitor by default, tag any variant that does name one, and route those to a human gate that is separate from the normal approve button. This is a switch in the Positioning Constitution, not a prompt suggestion.

04Persistence Score: the winner detector

One number per competitor ad, recomputed every sweep, from signals we can actually observe. Weights are a starting point to be tuned once we have our own outcome data to calibrate against.

SignalWhat it meansWeight
Days live, log scaledSurvived the kill decision. The strongest single signal.0.35
Still active nowCurrently surviving, not historically survived.0.15
Sibling countNumber of near-duplicate creatives in the same concept cluster. Buyers only iterate on winners, so a concept with 9 variants is a concept that works.0.20
Placement and surface breadthFeed plus Reels plus Stories, or Search plus Display plus YouTube. Scaling out costs money, so it signals confidence.0.10
Geo breadth, with the suppression caveatRegions where impressions were recorded. Treated as directional only.0.08
ResurrectionWent dark, then came back. A strong, rare and underused signal.0.07
Spend proxyAd count on this concept as a share of that advertiser's total live ads.0.05

Two derived views matter more than the raw score:

  • Half life by angle. Median days live for every ad in an angle cluster. Tells us which arguments have durability rather than which single ad got lucky.
  • Velocity. Net new ads per angle per week. Catches a competitor leaning into something before the longevity signal has time to appear, which is exactly what matters in a BFCM ramp.

05The angle inventory

Every ingested ad, static or video, goes through one vision and text pass that returns a structured object. This is the schema that everything downstream depends on, so it is worth over-thinking once and then freezing.

{
  "angle":        "pricing-fairness",        // the argument, clustered
  "promise":      "stop paying for contacts you never email",
  "mechanism":    "billing on sends, not stored profiles",
  "audience_cue": "scaling DTC brand with a bloated list",
  "pain":         "bill grew faster than revenue",
  "objection":    "migration is painful",
  "proof_type":   "price-comparison | screenshot | stat | testimonial | logo-wall | demo | none",
  "offer":        "free tier to 100k contacts",
  "cta":          "compare your bill",
  "format":       "static | video | carousel | text | display",
  "visual_world": "invoice / spreadsheet / receipt",
  "hook_text":    "Your list is not your bill.",
  "hook_seconds": 2.4,                        // video only
  "emotion":      "indignation",
  "names_competitor": true,
  "seasonal_tag": "bfcm"
}

Clustering into angles should be embedding based over the promise plus mechanism fields, with a human-curatable label per cluster. Do not let the model invent a fresh angle name per ad or the inventory becomes unusable within two sweeps.

The outputs that make this worth doing:

  • Share of voice by angle, per competitor, per week. The headline chart of the whole product.
  • Whitespace. Angles present in the ecommerce merchant conversation, from reviews and communities, that nobody is running ads on. The most valuable single screen for a brand marketer.
  • Angle diff. Entered, exited, accelerating, decaying, per competitor, week over week.
  • Attack map. Who names whom. Which of our own weaknesses competitors are hammering.

06The Positioning Constitution

The single most important file in the repo, and the one that stops this becoming a machine for producing off-brand slop at scale. Every generated ad is validated against it before it can reach a draft. It is a checked-in document with a machine-readable validator, not a paragraph in a system prompt.

Hard constraints

  • Category leads. Brevo ecom is a CRM and marketing platform. The AI ad and video wedge is proof and surprise, never the headline. Leading with ads re-shelves us into ad tech.
  • Never scrappy. No "small team", no underdog, no two-person framing, in any variant, ever.
  • No invented numbers. No ROAS, CTR, lift percentage, rating, award or customer count that is not on an approved claims ledger. This is the rule that broke twice in the prior pipeline and got a fabrication gate built for it. Reuse that gate.
  • Competitor naming is off by default and every naming variant is quarantined behind a separate legal gate.
  • No fake social proof. Testimonials come from a real approved list or are omitted.

Positive direction

  • Outcome carries: lifetime customers, not sends.
  • Pricing fairness is the sharpest wedge and gets the most variants.
  • Deliverability and peak-week reliability is the credibility angle.
  • Hook legible at thumbnail size, generous type, safe area respected.

The validator runs twice: on the copy before rendering, and as a vision pass on the rendered image, checking for unauthorised text, invented claims and misspellings. That second pass already exists and already caught a hallucinated money-back guarantee, so it should be lifted straight across.

07The six agent loops

Do not build one agent. Build six narrow ones behind a queue, each with a single responsibility and a checkable output. The chat console is a seventh that orchestrates them on demand.

Loop 1 · Sweep (weekly cron, plus on demand)

For each tracked brand, pull Meta page ads and Google advertiser ads. Diff against what we already hold. Write only new and changed rows. Update last_seen and mark disappearances. Enumerate all legal entities per domain, not just the first. On-demand mode accepts any brand name, domain or page id we have never tracked, resolves it, and runs the same path.

Loop 2 · Decompose

Vision and transcript pass over new creatives only. Statics go to a vision model, Meta videos through the ad transcript endpoint, Google video ads through the YouTube URL then transcript. Emits the angle object above. Costs money, so it runs once per creative, ever.

Loop 3 · Score and cluster

Recompute Persistence Score across the whole corpus, recluster angles, compute weekly deltas, detect entered and exited angles, write the digest.

Loop 4 · Adapt

Take the top scoring angles, filter for ICP fit against our funnel problem, run them through the Constitution, and emit briefs: the argument restated in our voice, with the hook variants, the proof we are allowed to use, and the format recommendation. This is a text-only loop and it is the one whose output a human should review most closely, because everything downstream inherits its mistakes.

Loop 5 · Produce

Statics rendered end to end from the existing template library and bake pipeline, with the fabrication gate and edge QA. Videos produce a shootable brief, not a file. Output lands in the gallery as a candidate, never as a draft.

Loop 6 · Measure

Pull our own Meta insights daily, join live ads back to the angle they descended from, compute per-angle and per-hook performance, detect fatigue, and feed the results back into the Adapt loop's ranking. This is the loop that makes the system compound, and it is the one most likely to get deferred. Do not defer it.

09Static and video generation

Static: the reverse-engineering method

This is the core of the production layer, and it is deliberately not "show the model a competitor ad and ask for something similar". That produces either plagiarism or mush. Six stages, each with a checkable output.

1 · Teardown into two layers, never prose

One vision pass per competitor static returns two objects. The argument layer is the angle schema from section 05. The craft layer is what actually stops the scroll, and it must be captured as structure:

{
  "device":        "annotated-invoice",     // the visual trick carrying the ad
  "zones": [
    {"role":"hook",    "x":0.06,"y":0.05,"w":0.88,"h":0.18,"z":1},
    {"role":"artifact","x":0.10,"y":0.26,"w":0.80,"h":0.48,"z":2},
    {"role":"callout", "x":0.55,"y":0.44,"w":0.34,"h":0.10,"z":3},
    {"role":"cta",     "x":0.06,"y":0.86,"w":0.50,"h":0.08,"z":4}
  ],
  "hierarchy":     ["hook","callout","artifact","cta","logo"],
  "type_system":   {"sizes":3,"weight_contrast":"900/400","case":"sentence",
                    "hook_height_pct":0.14},
  "palette":       ["#0B1F3A","#F4F1EA","#E23D2C"],  // sampled from the pixels
  "hook_contrast": 11.2,                              // ratio against its backdrop
  "negative_space":0.31,
  "proof_artifact":"invoice line item with a circled overage charge",
  "focal_points":  1
}

The lesson from the previous pipeline is load-bearing here: one-line descriptions rendered ads that read as obviously generated, and only a rich zoned spec carrying positions, type system and copy architecture produced work worth shipping. So the teardown is structural from the start.

2 · Abstract to a format

Strip the brand, the product and the claim. What survives is portable: annotated invoice, one circled line item, single red callout, hook above, sentence case, one focal point. That is a format. Formats are deduped across all ten competitors into a shared bank, each carrying the Persistence Score distribution of every ad built on it. A format used by four competitors with a median 70 days live is a validated container, independent of what anyone put inside it.

3 · Re-ground in Brevo, in a fixed order

Order matters because each step constrains the next.

  1. Argument is replaced with one of our angles from the Constitution. Never the competitor's claim rewritten.
  2. Proof artifact is replaced with a real Brevo artifact: an actual pricing table, a real UI capture, a number from the claims ledger. This is the single step where fabrication would enter the system, so it is gated: if no real artifact exists for that slot, the slot is dropped or the format is substituted for one that does not need it. The prior pipeline invented a money-back guarantee twice for a store with no returns policy, which is exactly the failure this gate exists to stop.
  3. Brand system comes from the brand kit: palette, type, logo lockup, safe area. The logo is composited from the real asset through a chroma slot rather than drawn by the model, because models reliably corrupt wordmarks.

4 · Uplift, deliberately

A re-skin is not the goal. Every adapted ad runs a craft uplift pass with explicit targets rather than vague instruction: hook type raised to the thumbnail-legible floor, exactly one focal point, hook contrast above threshold, fewer distinct type sizes than the source, more negative space than the source, and a rendered world in place of anything that reads as stock. The result is then scored against the source on the same craft rubric, and only variants that beat the source are kept. This is what turns "copy wisely" into something measurable instead of aspirational.

5 · Gates

Copy validator before render, against the Constitution and the claims ledger. Vision QA after render for unauthorised text, invented claims, logo fidelity, misspellings and thumbnail legibility. Safe-area and edge check. One hardened retry, then the candidate is dropped rather than shipped degraded.

6 · Ladder, not tweaks

Each approved brief produces the same argument across several different formats, not several nudges of one. Six to ten finished candidates per brief, so the review step is a real selection rather than take-it-or-leave-it. Sizes: Meta feed 1:1 and 4:5, Stories and Reels 9:16, Google Display in the standard set.

Google text and search

Fully generatable and cheap: 15 headlines and 4 descriptions per responsive search ad, built from the angle's promise and objection fields, plus sitelinks and callouts. Worth mining competitor text ads hard, because that copy is the most honest statement of a competitor's positioning they ever publish, and almost nobody analyses it.

Video, brief only

The output object is a shootable brief, and it should be graded on producibility so the pipeline is honest about what it is handing over:

  • AI complete Rendered end to end by a video model. Realistically: abstract or product-world concept films, kinetic typography, data and comparison pieces. Suitable for hook tests, not for anything needing a specific human face saying a specific line.
  • AI plus assets Needs a screen recording of the actual Brevo UI, a product shot, or a logo. The brief states precisely what to capture and at what length.
  • Human required Needs a person, a customer, or a real scenario. The brief becomes a creator brief, which plugs into the creator programme rather than the ad pipeline.

The brief carries: the hook as on-screen text and as spoken line, a beat sheet with timecodes, shot list, b-roll list, the asset request addressed to a human, the source competitor video it was derived from with its timestamped hook, and the aspect ratios to cut. The highest-leverage input here is the competitor hook library built from transcripts: the first three seconds of every high-persistence competitor video, transcribed and clustered. That library is a standalone asset, useful even if nothing else ships.

10Draft delivery

Nothing reaches a platform without an explicit human click. Approve in the gallery is a review action. Push to platform is a separate, deliberate second action. The agent never pushes on a cron, and never unpauses anything. This is a hard gate in code, not a convention.

Meta

Upload image to the ad account image library, create the ad creative, create the ad against a reserved paused campaign and ad set named AGENT DRAFT | angle | date, with status: PAUSED at every level. The human opens Ads Manager, sees the real rendered preview across placements, and either flips it live or deletes it. Record the returned platform ad id on our creative row immediately, because that id is the key the Measure loop joins on later.

Google

Use real CampaignDraft objects for structural changes, and paused ad groups with paused responsive search ads for creative. Upload display images as assets. Take the real-time policy verdict on responsive search ads and surface it in the gallery before a human spends attention on a creative that will be disapproved anyway.

13Alerts

Push, not pull. Weekly digest by default, immediate for the sharp ones.

  • New angle detected that no competitor ran before
  • A competitor's ad count jumps more than 40 percent week over week, which is a budget move
  • A competitor starts bidding on our brand terms
  • A competitor kills an angle we copied within the last 30 days
  • A competitor ad crosses 90 days live, the strong-winner threshold
  • One of our live creatives drops below its cohort median CTR, with a suggested replacement already generated
  • A draft has been sitting unreviewed for seven days

14The chat console

One text box on the home page, an agent loop with tools over the same D1 and R2. It should cover three request classes, and it is worth being explicit about them because they need different handling:

  1. Questions. "What is our CPA on the pricing angle this month." Answered from the database, with the query shown so the number is auditable.
  2. Actions. "Sweep Klaviyo and Omnisend for BFCM and make me five statics on their strongest offer angle." Enqueues real work, returns a job the user can watch, and lands the output in the gallery.
  3. Judgment. "Should we go after the deliverability angle." Answered with evidence from the corpus and an explicit recommendation, not a list of considerations.

Two design rules. First, every answer that contains a number links to the rows behind it, or the console becomes untrustworthy the first time it is subtly wrong. Second, any action that would write to Meta or Google returns a confirmation card rather than doing it, no exceptions, even when the request sounds unambiguous.

Open questions

The four questions this note originally raised are all settled, in the decisions table in section 02. What remains open:

  1. Whether the free ad-library routes return delivery start date and active status at the quality the Persistence Score needs. Tested in Phase 0, and the answer decides the sourcing budget for the whole system.
  2. Whether to give the agent's drafts a dedicated campaign structure inside the PushOwl account or a dedicated ad account, which matters for learning-phase contamination once volume rises. Not urgent while volume is low.
