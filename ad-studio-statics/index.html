Twelve findings

  1. Our own category has nearly left the static lane. Of 20 AI ad and video tools, 7 run no ads on their own page (MakeUGC, Icon, Pencil, Higgsfield, Atria, TopView, Pippit) and 4 run video only (Creatify, AdCreative.ai, Captions, Cuttable). 2. The framework

A rulebook for Ad Studio statics. Minimal means one idea at full volume, not a quiet idea. Every rule below comes from a legend, the research wall or the claims we can prove.

2.1 The big idea for the whole set: "The end of guessing"

Every ad declares one old way of making ads over, and shows the new way with a literal fact. The old ways: testing ads on your own money, starting from a blank canvas, writing briefs and booking shoots, mailing product to creators, and "free" trials that want your card. The new way is always one of the four things Ad Studio actually does:

  1. Clone any winning ad. Ad Radar shows the ads brands in your category keep paying for. Most ads stop within weeks; the ones still running months later are the winners. Pick one and rebuild it around your product.
  2. Static, video and UGC from one product link. Paste a link; the agent reads the page, your reviews and competitors, shows you the plan, then makes the ads.
  3. Your first 3 ads free, no credit card. One-time, no trial clock, image or video.
  4. Your first UGC creator free. Real-looking AI presenters show, use and review your product on camera.

The USP in one line (Reeves): start from ads that already won, made from your product link. Every one of the 20 sells one of the four pillars and nothing else.

The tone (from the brief): over the top in the frame, literal in the facts. A crash test dummy, a crime scene or a ring light in a museum is allowed. "10x your ROAS" is not.

2.3 The ten rules

  1. One idea per ad. If the headline needs "and", it's two ads.
  2. The headline does the selling. 8 words or fewer, readable alone in under two seconds, a benefit or a fact. Never a pun that hides what we sell.
  3. Specific beats superlative. Numbers come from the claims ledger. Never "best", "#1", "first", "only" or "revolutionary".
  4. Show the product or its output. A link, an Ad Radar tag, a finished ad or an AI presenter is on every canvas.
  5. Minimal, not empty. One focal object, 35% or more empty space, about 20 words on the image at most. Every ad still needs one surprising thing: an object, a number, a declaration or an admission.
  6. Over the top in the frame, literal in the facts. See 2.5.
  7. Name the old way, never a competitor. No rival names or real rival ads on the canvas. Demo ads use fictional brands tagged EXAMPLE.
  8. A fixed brand frame. Lockup "Brevo Ad Studio" bottom left, offer chip bottom right, CTA "Start for free". USD only. Never the words Shopify or Klaviyo. English only.
  9. Match the awareness level. The colder the viewer, the bigger the interruption; the warmer, the plainer the offer (2.6).
  10. Write the feed copy too. The primary text does the long-copy job Ogilvy loved (full sentences, contractions); the headline field restates the offer in under 40 characters; the button is Sign up.

2.3b Six rules the research adds

  1. A visible transformation can carry zero words. If the before and after explain themselves, cut the headline (Photoroom's best static has none).
  2. Keep exactly one concrete anchor. A number, a named job or the visible output. Mood alone needs a Claude-sized budget.
  3. Lead with free finished ads, and print "No credit card" on the image. Nobody in 73 brands does it; everyone else hides free in a button or a trial.
  4. One fake native screen in the set, at most. Our niche has worn them out (17 of 50).
  5. Answer the control fear. Where it fits, say you approve the plan before a credit is spent; the category's mood is "your taste, AI multiplies it".
  6. Treat statics as concept tests. Ship each as a 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 set, cut losers fast, and let survivors run for months with fresh headlines on the same picture.

2.5 Over the top, but credible

  1. Exaggerate the frame, never the fact. Staging can be theatrical (a crime scene, a museum, a protest). Numbers and capabilities stay literal.
  2. Every big line gets its mechanism in the subline. "Let your competitors pay for the testing" is followed by how: Ad Radar shows the ads they've kept running for months.
  3. Declare endings of old ways, not wins over rivals. "The blank canvas is dead" is a revolution. "Better than X" is a comparison nobody believes.
  4. Honesty is the weapon. Say "AI presenter" out loud. Say "no credit card" plainly. The category hides both, so saying them is distinctive.
  5. Use real numbers. Every day count on these ads comes from the public Ad Radar library (ads.brevo.com/ad-library/beauty, 12 Nov 2026): 691, 336, 314, 286 and 274 days.
  6. No promise of results. No ROAS, no "sells more", no guaranteed anything. Case results only with the brand named, exactly as on the site.

Appendix: Batch 2, copy-led provocation (ads 21 to 40)

What changes from batch 1. Batch 1 won on one big visual idea. Batch 2 wins on copy alone: quiet layouts, one focal element, and headlines a media buyer would screenshot. Every ad sells one offer: clone 3 winning ads, free. Paste a product link, pick an ad your competitors have run for months on Ad Radar, and Brevo Ad Studio rebuilds it around your product, brand and offer. No credit card.

The research behind it. 51 provocative statics: 36 pulled live from the Meta Ad Library on 13 Nov 2026 and 15 from the batch 1 wall. The famous provocateurs (Artisan, Ryanair, MSCHF, Clay, Friend) run no edgy Meta statics; their provocation lives on billboards. The strongest live example is Vanta's rival-talking series ("SH*******T. THEY'RE ALREADY IN ENTERPRISE PILOTS?"), flat colour and no product shot. The banger test (score 0 to 10 on each; ship only an average of 8 or more)

  1. Stops the thumb in half a second.
  2. One idea.
  3. The offer (clone 3 winning ads, free) is clear in 3 seconds without the caption.
  4. True and provable: every number traces to Ad Radar or a real clone run.
  5. Makes waiting feel costly.
  6. A media buyer would screenshot it and send it to a friend.

44 headlines written, scored, 20 kept. Every kept headline scores 8.2 or more; the bank and the scores are on the Batch 2 page.

Edge guardrails

  • Urgency without lies. The offer has no end date, so urgency comes from loss and rivalry: a rival's ad has run 691 days and counts on, every day of guessing is spend, Black Friday is on the calendar. No countdowns, no "today only", no invented scarcity, and never the word deal.
  • Real, not dummy. Every "before" is a real ad from Ad Radar, with its run length read from the public Ad Radar library on 12 Nov 2026, its Ad Radar page and its Meta ad ID. Every "after" is a real Clone run in Ad Studio on 13 Nov 2026 (about 80 to 113 seconds each), shown as Ad Studio produced it. Third-party brand names, logos and a reviewer's name are blurred.
  • Clone means rebuild. Every clone line says it is rebuilt for or around your product. Never "steal", never imply copying someone's assets.
  • Borderline, not over. Cheeky, confrontational, a little rude about agencies, guessing and screenshots. Never the reader's identity, never profanity, never a named competitor, never shock without the offer.
  • House rules. No long dashes. Never Shopify or Klaviyo. USD only, no country cues. English only. Type rendered by a real image model; only the logo and the real artifacts are composited.
