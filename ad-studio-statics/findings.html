Twelve findings

  1. Our own category has nearly left the static lane. Of 20 AI ad and video tools, 7 run no ads on their own page (MakeUGC, Icon, Pencil, Higgsfield, Atria, TopView, Pippit) and 4 run video only (Creatify, AdCreative.ai, Captions, Cuttable). The statics that do run come from tools sold to media buyers (Madgicx, Motion, Foreplay, Arcads) and to small businesses (Predis, Zeely, HeyGen). A strong static from Ad Studio has little direct competition in the feed.
  2. Before and after is the workhorse. 22 of the 72 creative-tool statics use it, in eight layouts. The strongest for stores: a small inset of the plain phone photo next to a big finished result, and no headline at all. When the transformation is visible, the words can go. [[ev:1754093965672896,3017693548573934]]
  3. "One in, many out" is the headline closest to our promise, and nobody shows all three formats. Canva's "One product photo. A week of posts." and Runway's 24-tile variant grid fan one input into many outputs, but every brand shows a single output type. No static shows a link turning into a static, a video and an AI presenter at once. [[ev:1859241038420774,1072646035738492]]
  4. The prompt is the headline. ChatGPT, Otter, Copilot, Bolt and Canva put one white chat bar with the user's own words over a photo or the output. It reads as "AI" in a blink and hands the viewer the exact words to type. No AI ad tool uses it yet. [[ev:1928407375244309,3985944458209892,1138385022290391]]
  5. Price shown as a document is the most-scaled static in the category. HeyGen's "INVOICE: One year of weekly videos" ($41,600 vs $24/month) runs as 19 variants. Fireflies sets "$144/year" against "$0/year"; Gamma runs "PowerPoint: 4 hours. Gamma: 4 minutes." Nobody prices a static ad in USD. [[ev:1387727206022106,2332266970940867,1103579118735850]]
  6. Minimal works only with one concrete anchor. A number, a named job or platform, or the visible output. "Your Poshmark closet. Sold." (Perplexity), "One API key." (Hedra) and "You bake. We post." (Predis) are one line and a logo. Pure mood (Claude's "There's more to discover.") needs big budgets and 20+ variants to work. The long runners sit at 3 to 4 out of 5 on minimalism: one claim, one proof visual, one soft CTA. [[ev:2591136484654769,1443190717963112,3612671548881097]]
  7. "Free" is a button, a trial, a report or a webinar. Never free finished ads. Madgicx prints a 7-day trial, Motion gives away a report, Zeely a webinar, Suno shows a $0 tier. Only ClickUp puts "no credit card" on the image. Nobody leads with "your first ads are free". That makes our offer news.
  8. Fake native screens are now wallpaper in the ad-tool niche. 17 of 50 statics there fake a LinkedIn post, WhatsApp group, IG story, chat or code editor. They work, but in our category they no longer stand out. We use one at most.
  9. Long runners are evergreen, launch creative is disposable. A Zapier carousel has run 924 days, Airtable lead magnets 231, a Firefly poster 286, ElevenLabs' "From Text to Audiobook in One Click" 267. Consumer AI brands rotate statics every 2 to 8 weeks and use them as cheap concept tests; survivors then run for 6 to 9 months. [[ev:416986901046052,879990861302304,1425331672161988]]
  10. The mood moved from "AI does it for you" to "your taste, AI multiplies it". Canva runs "Drop the slop", Runway "Your creative team makes the hero. Runway makes the rest." Human control is now proof: Runway Ads prints "Nothing goes live without your approval." Ad Studio's plan approval ("you approve before it spends a credit") answers the same fear. [[ev:1094720753339541]]
  11. Direct competitors are moving onto our ground. Runway Ads (launched 30 Sep) sells "upload an ad you already run, get on-brand variants back". Higgsfield launched Marketing Studio in October (product page to finished campaign) but runs zero ads. Foreplay shows a Glossier ad "running 315 days" and then stops at research. Nobody yet puts a competitor's long-running ad next to "your version". That is ours to take.
  12. Ship every static as a 3-size set. The leaders deliver one creative as 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 cards in one DCO unit (Copilot runs one static across about 15 ad IDs, Lovable across 13). Our 4:5 master with a square-safe centre and a 9:16 cut matches that.

What the research changed in the concepts

  • 03 moved from Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V keycaps to a photocopier: Arcads already runs the keycaps (ad 872873428672343).
  • 11 moved from a cardboard protest sign to a megaphone revolution poster: Canva runs the protest-sign meme (ad 2367846104025813).
  • 16 was an iMessage thread. Fake chats are the most common device in our niche and it scored 78, under the bar, so it became a prompt bar carrying the store owner's Black Friday job and showing three formats.
  • 14 gained a USD price line ($19 for 50+ statics and 5+ videos), because no ad tool prices a static.
  • 06, 10, 16 show a competitor's long-running ad next to "your version", or one link turning into all three formats. Both are white space no brand in the research set fills.

Print and outdoor worth knowing (2025 to 2026)

  • Canva, "Solving everyday design dramas" (Waterloo, London; ADC Bronze Cube and One Show Silver Pencil 2026). Each board demos one feature as a design in-joke from the audience's own work. The medium is the demo.
  • ChatGPT. India boards (Sept 2025) showed only a prompt on a plain field. "Reimagine anything" (2026) boards appeared to edit the building they hung on. An earlier run of mundane slice-of-life boards confused people: minimal without an anchor fails, even for ChatGPT.
  • Claude, "Keep thinking" (Mother London). Real people, warm palette, serif type, no robots. It stands out because the category is full of glowing brains. Named Best B2B Campaign at Ad Age's 2026 Creativity Awards.
  • Notion and Granola. Both chose a hand-made, anti-AI look on purpose, so their boards stand out in a row of AI logos.
