Every card shows: the action, the reason in one line, the expected impact ($ or CPA), a confidence score, the evidence (a chart and the numbers), and buttons for Approve, Dismiss with a reason, or Snooze. Nothing needs your targets except the thresholds: target CPA and monthly budget per platform (decision 1).

The brain: how it learns without drifting

Ledger, not a model

Every recommendation, your decision and the outcome measured 3 and 7 days later is written as a row. Same evidence in, same recommendation out. This is the lesson from the clustering bug.

Priors that move

Win rates per angle, genre, hook type, format and narrative, updated from real results and weighted by spend. These priors steer both the weekly creative mix and recommendation confidence.

Learns from your no

A dismissal with a reason ("seasonal", "brand risk") suppresses similar recommendations. A rule dismissed 3 times gets its threshold flagged for you to review.

Readable

A "What I've learned" panel lists each learning with its evidence count, its confidence, and the date it last changed. You can pin a learning or overrule it.

6. Storyboard pipeline for video concepts You click Storyboard on a tile. The agent writes everything and renders every sheet. You only paste into Seedance and generate.

Concept→Script→Screenplay→Clip breakdown→Asset plan→Sheets rendered→Keyframes rendered→Seedance run sheet→Edit guide
StageWhat it producesRules
1. ScriptHook (0 to 3s), problem, turn, proof, CTA. Spoken VO and on-screen text are written separately.Carries the concept's narrative, angle and claim status. A placeholder figure stays marked. Klaviyo is named only in the tagged bucket, and no other competitor is ever named.
2. ScreenplayScenes with location, time, characters, action, dialogue or VO, and mood.Only fictional characters, never a real person's likeness.
3. Clip breakdownEach scene split into clips of 5 to 10 seconds. Per clip: shot size, lens feel, camera move (dolly in, orbit, handheld push, whip pan), subject action, transition in and out, audio.Each clip does one motion job. Seedance holds identity best over short clips.
4. Asset planThe list of reference assets the clips need, with which clip uses each.Seedance takes at most 9 images per generation. A plan that needs more is split.
5. Sheets renderedCharacter sheet (turnaround + expressions, for our own continuity), character identity card (one front view, for Seedance), product/UI sheet (real Brevo UI from our 30 official screens), location sheet, prop sheet, contact sheet (every clip's frame on one page, for sign-off).Guides warn that Seedance reads a multi-view sheet as several different people. So Seedance gets the single view identity card; the turnaround is only used to render our keyframes.
6. Keyframes renderedA start frame for every clip, and an end frame where the clip must land somewhere exact.Rendered from the sheets, so the face, wardrobe and set match across clips. This is the main consistency lever: first frame mode anchors each clip.
7. Seedance run sheetNumbered steps per clip: the files to upload in order (the most important first, because order carries weight), the mode (first frame, or first + last frame), duration, aspect, and the prompt with copy buttons.Prompt shape: subject from which image, the action, the camera move, the style, what must stay unchanged, the audio. References are named the way your host expects ("Image 1" or "@Image1", set once in settings).
8. Edit guideClip order, trims, on-screen text and captions (added in the edit, not generated), end card with the real wordmark, music cues, and the 16:9 in-stream and 9:16 Shorts cuts.Text and logos are never left to the video model. Same rule as the statics.

In the tile, the Storyboard panel expands below the card with 6 tabs: Script, Screenplay, Clips, Sheets (image grid with download), Run sheet (numbered and copyable), and Edit. There's also a Copy all button and a ZIP download. It renders in the background with a progress bar, taking about 8 to 14 image renders per concept. You can re-render any single sheet or keyframe. After you generate a clip, you can upload it back to the tile, and the agent checks it against its keyframe and suggests a prompt fix if it drifted.
