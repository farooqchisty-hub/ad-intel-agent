The idea in one line: every email reads like a short note from Pranavi to one store owner who asked for the deal: it gets to the point in the first line, explains one thing in plain words, asks for one action, and uses only real deadlines. The emails pick up from the ad the person clicked, so the first email continues the conversation instead of starting a new one.

1. Who's reading

  • Store owners who clicked a Black Friday ad and typed their email to unlock the code. They already want the deal; the sequence's job is to get them installed and to the Plans page before 30 Nov.
  • It's their busiest season, so they skim on their phones between other tasks. Short beats clever.
  • Most of them don't know Pranavi yet. Trust comes from being specific and useful, not from enthusiasm.

2. Pranavi's voice

She sounds like
  • A teammate who helps new stores get set up, writing to one person
  • "I" for herself, "we" only when the team does something (we'll move your lists)
  • Calm and direct. She tells you the next step and why it's worth it
  • Helpful even when you don't buy (a tip, an honest answer)
She never sounds like
  • A promo blast ("Don't miss out!!!", "Huge savings inside")
  • A corporate notice ("We are pleased to inform you")
  • A salesperson pushing ("Act now before it's too late")
  • An AI ("Unlock the power of seamless omnichannel engagement")

4. Subject lines

What the research says

  • Be specific about the offer. In a study of 4.2 billion Black Friday and Cyber Monday emails, subject lines with a clear, specific discount did best; ALL CAPS did 15% worse than mixed case; multiple exclamation marks went with more spam complaints; and the fire and gift emojis went with 8% lower opens (Bird).
  • Keep it short, put the key words first. Gmail on Android shows about 33 characters (Simplelists, citing EmailToolTester); Klaviyo's 2025 data shows opens falling as lines get wordier (Klaviyo).
  • Sentence case or lowercase, never Title Case. Sentence case beat Title Case by about 6% once Apple's inflated opens were corrected for (Visionary Marketing). B2B guidance favours short lines that read like a message from a colleague (Instantly).
  • Emojis: skip them. In the same corrected data they lifted opens about 7% but cut clicks about 12% (Visionary Marketing). We care about clicks.
  • A person's name as sender tends to help. In one test, "Name at Company" won on clicks in 8 of 10 sends (9clouds). Hence "Pranavi at Brevo PushOwl".
  • Plain-looking emails do better in B2B. A widely cited HubSpot test on 100k+ B2B contacts found plain text earned higher opens, clicks and far more replies than designed templates (Outerbox summary, Stripo). Vendor data, so we test it rather than assume it.
  • Judge tests on clicks, not opens. Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens (Klaviyo).

Our rules

  • 25 to 45 characters, the important word in the first 30.
  • Lowercase "note from a colleague" style, except the code and the numbers.
  • No emojis, no exclamation marks, no ALL CAPS, no "FREE".
  • Preview text adds a second reason to open; it never repeats the subject.
  • Every email ships with two subjects: one plain "colleague" line and one specific offer line. Brevo splits the send where the workflow allows it; the winner is picked on clicks.

  • Frequency: never two emails within 48 hours, except D3. If a date email lands within 48 hours of an L or B email, the L or B email is skipped.

7. Deliverability and compliance

  • Domain: pushowl.com is verified in Brevo, signs with DKIM and has a DMARC record (p=none), which meets Gmail and Yahoo's sender basics. Brevo adds one-click unsubscribe.
  • Fresh list: these are new sign-ups who just asked for the deal, which is the best list you can send to. Low volume, low frequency.
  • Consent: everyone on the list asked for emails about the deal (the page says so). The new tick box covers other marketing. After 30 Nov, only people who ticked it get further marketing emails.
  • Stop-loss: if any email's unsubscribe rate goes over 1%, it's paused and rewritten.

8. What we measure

Per email: clicks, installs within 3 days (matched through Intercom), code applied, unsubscribes and replies. Subject tests are judged on clicks.
