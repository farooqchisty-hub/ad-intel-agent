BFCM 2026: twenty 60 s video concepts

Stage 1 of the cinematic video system for the Brevo PushOwl BFCM 2026 campaign: five one-minute video ads for each of the four narratives. About 8 raw ideas per narrative were scored and the best 5 kept. Concepts only: nothing has been generated and nothing spent. Written 8 Oct 2026 from CONTEXT.md (decisions 7 and 8 Oct), the messaging house, the approved v3 statics and the four finished teasers.

How to pick
  • Reply with the IDs you want storyboarded (for example "N1-1, N2-1, N3-1, N4-1"), plus any changes. Your edits override the card.
  • Recommended first wave: the four top picks, one per narrative, about $195 all-in. All 20: about $874.
  • Next gate: a storyboard per pick (cast cards, keyframes, unit prompts, sound and music plan, cost). No video is generated before you approve a storyboard.
Rules every concept follows
  • Two takes, native audio. Each 60 s ad is two continuous single-shot generations of about 30 s (Seedance 2.5 face via treg) with every word generated in the video; no TTS, no lip-sync, no voiceover over silent footage. Every spoken word below goes into the unit prompt in quotes with its timecode and delivery adjectives. Takes are generated with no music; one score is composed to the cut.
  • Dialogue cap. The harness validator allows 2.0 words a second; every new take here is at or under 55 words (counts on each take).
  • Brand. Spoken lines say only "PushOwl", prompted as Push-OWL with the proven phonetic note, once per video (twice at most) and only in take B, so take A re-rolls never fail on the brand word. The Brevo PushOwl lockup appears on screen only (end card). No platform or competitor names in any line or super: "your current email tool", "per-contact pricing", "ecommerce store owners".
  • Hook in 2 s. Every video opens on a plain line plus a big hook super and the audience tag "Ecommerce store owners" (the approved statics' register).
  • Close. Take B ends with the offer and the real deadline spoken, the action strip on screen ("50% off annual · 30% off monthly", "Code BFCM2026 · Ends 30 Nov", "Claim it" button), then a 3 to 4 s end card with the full offer. The code is on screen only, not spoken (open question 1).
  • Surface level. No credit counts, no "forever", no 15 Nov cut-off, no capacity or scarcity claims, no performance claims. N2 uses only X1 to X5 word for word, with its footnote on screen.
  • Text. Nothing readable is generated inside a frame (it smears): receipts, boards, screens, notes and slates are blank in generation and every word on screen is an overlay from the harness graphics templates or a real product capture. Screens face away from camera.
  • Cast and world. North American casting, no real people or celebrities, fictional store names on supplied boards, every other sign blank. Burned-in captions on every line for sound-off viewing.
What the production harness can and can't do

Makes reliably: 4 to 30 s continuous takes in 9:16 with native dialogue and ambience; recurring faces and voices from cast cards and voice refs; first-frame inserts for fixes; overlays from 9 templates (toast, message, product card, claim card, callout from real page captures, tracker, stamp, CTA, outro); one composed score; captions; frame-exact assembly; QA with two transcribers on the vocal stem, a frame scan and loudness. Does not do reliably: readable text inside generated frames; brand words in long takes (about 1 in 4, hence 3 to 4 rolls of take B); fast physics (spins, falls, flying objects); big crowds staying consistent; telling two men's voices apart in the voice check; busy sets without real-brand leaks (scan and crop); anything longer than 30 s in one generation.

How the concepts were scored

The cinematic skill fixes the card format but has no numeric rubric, so each idea is scored with the creative asset framework: six criteria at 0 to 10 each, out of 60 (Relevance to the narrative and offer, Scroll-stop in the first 2 s, Virality, Creativity, Catchiness, Uniqueness). The top 5 per narrative are kept; ties are broken by production risk (lower wins). The top pick per narrative is its highest score.
