What's wrong with the flat discount

  • It arrives after the buying window. Brevo's 2025 deal ran 19 Nov to 1 Dec (40% off the first annual year, 500 codes). Merchants pick and set up their BFCM tools in October to mid November; by Black Friday they're mid-sale and their stack is locked. Shopify apps that front-load (GemPages from 1 Oct, Easify's biggest cut in September) are following the buyer.
  • It gives cold merchants no reason to care. "40% off a tool I don't use" sells nothing to a stranger. Their problem is "is my store ready for my biggest week", not software prices.
  • It's background noise. Brevo, Omnisend, Framer, Kit, beehiiv and Webflow all ran "X% off annual with code BFXX". Coupon sites copy the codes, and in 2025 a Brevo affiliate code (50% off 3 months) beat our own headline.
  • Free users get nothing that matches why they'd upgrade. They upgrade when they hit a limit or need a feature for peak, not because a percentage exists.

What worked elsewhere (26 offers researched)

The three we're building on: give a service instead of a price cut, show the discount as free months, and front-load with a bonus that expires. They're the three gifts inside Peak's On Us.

Give a service, not a price cut

Semrush 2024: one month of Pro plus a free $300 consult by a partner agency, price untouched. Omnisend: free Klaviyo migration, "worth $1,000, done in 5 days", run all year. Free setup removes the real blocker for a busy merchant.

Show the discount as free months

MeetEdgar called 30% off a year "three months free"; ReturnPrime "2 months free"; Okendo "first 30 days free". Same money, reads as a gift instead of a markdown.

Cut the commitment for cold traffic

ElevenLabs ran "$1 first month" two years running. A cold stranger won't prepay a year; they'll try a month.

Bonus capacity for upgraders

Descript sold "fewer limits" (bonus credits), Higgsfield "unlimited". Free users feel limits first, so extra sends or credits speak to the exact reason they'd pay.

Free-user-only offers and early access

Kit limited its deal to new and free-plan users; Grammarly emails its free base every year ("only once a year"); Paddle recommends existing users get first access.

Partners carry it to cold audiences

PushOwl's own 2024 roundup of 20 partner-app deals; AppSumo's 5-tool bundle sold out. Every partner sends the offer to its own list.

Front-load, then add a reason to hurry

Early-bird bonuses that expire (DronaHQ: 40% to 15 Nov, then 30%). Better: keep one price and let a bonus expire, so early buyers are never undercut.

Evidence on discounting

Paddle: discounting SaaS sellers see about 7x orders, best at 30 to 40% off. Counterpoint (Hurman, ecommerce): deep discounts often lose margin; bonus value and waived setup hold up better.

Campaign 1 (recommended lead): "Peak's On Us"

The idea: your biggest month shouldn't come with your biggest bill or your biggest to-do list. We cover your peak months and set BFCM up for you. Same discount, packaged as three things a merchant actually wants before Black Friday.

1. Your peak months, free

The discount shown as months: e.g. Nov and Dec free on a monthly plan, or about 5 months free on annual (the 2025 40% annual equals ~4.8 months). On PushOwl this maps cleanly to a longer Shopify-billing free trial of Plus or Power (to confirm with engineering).

2. BFCM set up for you

PushOwl: a one-click BFCM pack (abandoned cart, browse abandonment, back in stock, flash-sale push, pop-up) live in 10 minutes. Brevo: a done-for-you BFCM setup or Klaviyo migration, delivered by Managed Services or Expert Directory partner agencies (N4).

3. A prep bonus that expires 15 Nov

Buy early, get more: e.g. 10 BFCM ads made from your store (the new ads feature as the wedge, N2), or extra sends/SMS credits for peak. The price never changes during the campaign; only the bonus expires.

Why this one leads: it answers the merchant's real question in the window when they're asking it, it uses the patterns with the most evidence (free months, free setup, early bonus), and it gives every channel the same simple promise.

Campaign 2 (cold-traffic wedge): "Your BFCM Ads, Done"

The idea: paste your store, get your Black Friday ads. A free, useful thing a cold merchant wants right now, which brings them into the product, where Campaign 1's upgrade offer takes over.

  • Offer: sign up or install and get a set of free BFCM ads made from your own products (the ads feature, N2's "ads from your store"). Upgrade within the window to keep generating and to get Peak's On Us.
  • Best on: Instagram and YouTube with marketer and AI-tool creators ("I made my Black Friday ads from my store URL in 2 minutes"), and as a Meta cold hook.
  • Care needed: our ad-corpus analysis showed AI-led ads die fast (15-day median) and N2 is our weakest narrative as a claim. So lead with the outcome ("your BFCM ads are done") and the free gift, not "AI".
  • Runs on both brands: PushOwl (Shopify) users and Brevo users both get the ads feature, so the same wedge works for both cold audiences.

The Campaigns tab for the Brevo team (second priority)

  • One card per campaign: objective, audience, KPIs, budget, schedule, destination and UTMs, naming conventions.
  • Every creative attached and grouped by platform (Meta images and videos, Google RSA text), with copy, download buttons and a Google Ads Editor CSV.
  • Status and notes they can read; a share link per campaign. Read-only for them: they run it in their own accounts their way.
  • Built after we pick the campaign, ready before 2 Nov.

Decisions for you

  • Pick the lead campaign (recommended: Peak's On Us, with Your BFCM Ads, Done as the cold wedge).
  • The real discount value and eligibility per brand, and whether PushOwl can deliver it as extended free trial days in Shopify billing.
  • Who delivers "set up for you": Managed Services, Expert Directory partners, or the one-click pack only.
  • Lift the Meta hold so the PushOwl warm-up can go live on 5 or 6 Oct.
  • Offer launch: 2 Nov (recommended) or 26 Oct. The warm-up starts 5 or 6 Oct either way.
  • Budgets per channel and brand, and the creator budget (book this week).
